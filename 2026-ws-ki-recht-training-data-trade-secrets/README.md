# Schutz von KI-Trainingsdaten als Geschäftsgeheimnis in Technologieunternehmen

*Protecting AI Training Data as Trade Secrets in Technology Companies*

**Course:** KI-Grundlagen, Teilmodul KI-Recht · **Semester:** WS 26/27 · **Language:** German

## Summary

When a company hands years of proprietary R&D data to an external developer to train an AI model, does that data stay legally protected? This paper examines which confidentiality measures are "appropriate" under the German Trade Secrets Act (GeschGehG), since taking such measures is itself a precondition for protection.

## Approach

Legal analysis of a fictional case: a technology company provides non-personal process and test data, including failed experiments, to an external developer who trains a defect-detection model. The analysis draws on the GeschGehG and its legislative materials, a 2024 ruling of the Federal Labour Court (BAG, 8 AZR 172/23), and practical guidance from Bitkom and the German data protection authorities (DSK).

## Key Findings

- Outsourcing AI development does not remove trade secret protection, but only if access rights, contractual purpose limitation, and internal rules are actually in place.
- In a dispute, the owner has to show specifically which measures were taken. Blanket references to IT security are not enough, so permissions and approvals should be documented.
- Training data may be extractable from the trained model, and reverse engineering is generally permitted under the GeschGehG. Contracts should therefore explicitly prohibit reconstructing training data from the model.
- Protection has limits: independent discovery is lawful, and once secrecy is lost, only damage claims remain. Preventive measures matter more than remedies after the fact.
